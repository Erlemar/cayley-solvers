# A diameter upper bound with linear coefficient C = 1

This bound is improved to C=3/4 in
[COMBINED_EXTRACTION_UPPER_BOUND.md](COMBINED_EXTRACTION_UPPER_BOUND.md).

For the Cayley graph on S_n generated by the wrapped consecutive
4-cycles and their inverses, the following upper bounds are proved:

\[
\boxed{D(n)\le
\left\lfloor\frac{\lfloor n^2/4\rfloor+3n+35}{3}\right\rfloor
\qquad(n\ge16).}
\tag{1}
\]

For every n >= 5, a uniform version is

\[
\boxed{D(n)\le
\left\lfloor\frac{\lfloor n^2/4\rfloor+3n+47}{3}\right\rfloor
\le\frac{n^2}{12}+n+\frac{47}{3}.}
\tag{2}
\]

This is an upper bound, not an exact diameter formula. The proof uses a
complete finite certificate of elementary permutation identities, plus
an argument valid for every n. Finite random experiments are supplementary
checks and are not the reason (1) holds for all n.

## 1. The final stage is a merge

Use the balanced-lift decomposition proved in
[LINEAR_UPPER_BOUND.md](LINEAR_UPPER_BOUND.md). Its three adjacent-swap
costs are I_1, q, I_2, with

\[
I_1+q+I_2\le K:=\lfloor n^2/4\rfloor.
\tag{3}
\]

The first stage sorts a linear interval of n entries. Its sorted lifted
values are z_0 < ... < z_{n-1}. The middle stage moves t=z_{n-1}=n-1+q
clockwise across q entries and fixes it. For q > 0, the remaining interval
of n-1 entries, starting at position q, is

\[
z_q,\ldots,z_{n-2},\quad z_0+n,\ldots,z_{q-1}+n.
\tag{4}
\]

Both displayed lists are increasing. When q=0 the remaining interval is
already increasing. Thus the final stage only has to merge two increasing
lists, rather than sort an arbitrary permutation.

## 2. A stable extraction for two increasing lists

Consider a list A B, with both A and B increasing. Choose its k smallest
entries. There are a of them at the beginning of A and b=k-a at the
beginning of B. Write g=|A|-a=3u+r, with 0 <= r <= 2.

Move each of the b chosen B-entries left across u triples of unchosen
A-entries. These bu inverse 4-cycles each remove exactly three inversions:
every chosen entry is smaller than every unchosen entry. Afterward the
prefix ending with the last chosen entry consists of

1. the a chosen A-entries;
2. r unchosen A-entries;
3. the b chosen B-entries.

The relative order of all unchosen entries is unchanged. Label the chosen
entries 0,...,k-1 in increasing order, and give the unchosen entries in
this prefix artificial labels k,...,k+r-1 in their positional order.
Let p be the resulting pattern and J its ordinary inversion number.

Record the source A or B of each chosen entry in increasing-value order
as a binary string sigma of length k. The pair (sigma,r) determines p:

\[
p=[\text{A-labelled indices of }\sigma],\ [k,\ldots,k+r-1],\
  [\text{B-labelled indices of }\sigma].
\tag{5}
\]

Sorting this artificial pattern stably extracts the k chosen entries.
The true inversion decrease Delta of the complete extraction satisfies

\[
\Delta=3bu+J.
\tag{6}
\]

Therefore, a local sorting word of length t with 3t-J <= k gives an
extraction of cost L=bu+t with

\[
3L-\Delta\le k.
\tag{7}
\]

## 3. The complete finite extraction certificate

Begin at k=3. The 24 possibilities are the eight binary strings sigma,
each with r=0,1,2. For each case, either use a word satisfying (7), or
increase k by selecting the next-smallest entry. There are precisely two
possible refinements:

\[
(\sigma,r)\longmapsto
\begin{cases}
(\sigma A,r-1\bmod3),\\
(\sigma B,r).
\end{cases}
\tag{8}
\]

Indeed, adding an A-entry reduces g by one, whereas adding a B-entry
leaves g unchanged. The choice of k is made before executing any moves;
there is no cost for refining a case. An impossible refinement, for
example selecting from an already exhausted A-list, can harmlessly be
included in the certificate.

The file [merge_extraction_c1.json](merge_extraction_c1.json) gives a
complete finite tree. Every terminal case has an explicit word, using
only nonwrapped 4-cycles within max(9,k+r) <= 16 consecutive positions.
Positions beyond the pattern are temporary helpers, assigned increasing
artificial labels. The sorting identity restores them exactly.

All branches terminate by k=14. The independent checker in

discover the words is not needed to verify the certificate. No assertion
that a nonterminal case lacks a shorter word is used in the proof.

## 4. The all-length merge lemma and boundary helpers

After a stable extraction, the remaining entries still form two
increasing lists. Repeat until they are sorted.

For the last batch, if fewer than 16 entries remain, use the following
already fixed cyclic positions as temporary helpers. Assign them
artificial ranks greater than all remaining entries, in their current
order, and regard them as belonging to the end of B. There are 16
distinct positions available when n >= 16. The local permutation
identities restore all helpers, regardless of their actual lifted values.

If the selected k exceeds the number h of actual remaining entries,
then every real A-entry has already been selected, so g=0. Thus no
preparatory move crosses a helper, and the inversion-decrease identity
(6) still counts exactly the real inversions removed. Only the last
batch can charge helpers. A nontrivial batch starts with h >= 2 and
selects k <= 14, so at most 12 helpers are charged.

Summing (7) over the batches proves, for a merge interval of length m
and inversion number I,

\[
\boxed{3L_{\rm merge}\le I+m+12.}
\tag{9}
\]

This assertion allows surrounding positions as temporary helpers and
requires at least 16 distinct cyclic positions in total. It does not
assert the same bound for a short isolated interval without helpers.

## 5. Combining the stages

The arbitrary-interval sorting lemma in
[ADAPTIVE_UPPER_BOUND.md](ADAPTIVE_UPPER_BOUND.md) gives

\[
3L_1\le I_1+2n+10.
\]

The single-token middle stage satisfies 3L_0 <= q+14. By (9), the last
stage of length n-1 satisfies 3L_2 <= I_2+(n-1)+12. Hence

\[
3(L_1+L_0+L_2)
\le I_1+q+I_2+3n+35
\le K+3n+35.
\]

This proves (1). For 5 <= n <= 15, the previous all-n bound
3D(n) <= K+4n+32 implies 3D(n) <= K+3n+47. Combining this with (1)
proves (2), without relying on unverified small diameter values.

The checks in [merge_sort_checks.json](merge_sort_checks.json) include
all 396 abstract cases, all 8188 binary merges of lengths 2 through 12
with restored helpers, and independent replay of 1656 complete words
at sizes 16 through 150, 250, 500, and 1000.

The exact diameter and its sharp linear term remain open in this work.
