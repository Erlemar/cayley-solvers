# Critical analysis of `Cayleypy_diameter_bounding.pdf`

Date reviewed: 2026-07-11

## Executive verdict

The central result for generators consisting only of disjoint 2- and 3-cycles,

\[
D^n_{m_2,m_3}=\frac{n}{m_2+2m_3}+O(1),
\]

is mathematically credible and follows once Theorem 4 is repaired. The lower bound
(Theorem 2), the scaling idea (Theorem 7), and the final asymptotic corollary all have the
right leading term.

The document is not yet a complete proof suitable for circulation:

1. Lemma 6, one of the two base cases needed for the corollary, has no proof.
2. The proof of Theorem 4 applies a diameter bound to block permutations without ensuring
   that they lie in the subgroup generated on that block. This is a real parity gap when a
   component conjugacy class generates an alternating group.
3. Theorem 4 also uses, without proof, the fact that an element with bounded support has
   uniformly bounded word length as `n` grows.
4. Several statements omit ceilings and stable-range conditions (`n` must at least contain
   the support of a generator).
5. Expressions such as `D >= n/K + O(1)` should be written `D >= n/K - O(1)` or with an
   explicit bounded constant.

The gaps in Theorem 4 are repairable. They do not appear to invalidate the theorem. The
strongest new conclusion from the literature check is negative: the transposition-rank
denominator `S'` does **not** give the correct leading constant for cycles of length at
least 4. Exact cycle-class covering results yield different, genuinely periodic formulas.

## 1. Cleaner setup

Fix a nonidentity cycle type

\[
\lambda=(2^{m_2}3^{m_3}\cdots k^{m_k}),
\]

and define

\[
S(\lambda)=\sum_{j\ge2}j m_j,
\qquad
R(\lambda)=\sum_{j\ge2}(j-1)m_j.
\]

The paper calls these `S` and `S'`. The second quantity has a standard interpretation:

\[
R(\sigma)=n-c(\sigma),
\]

where `c(sigma)` counts all cycles, including fixed points. It is the exact transposition
length of `sigma`. Calling `R` the **index** or **absolute/transposition length** makes the
proofs more transparent.

The class `C^n_lambda` exists only for `n >= S(lambda)`. Its sign is

\[
\operatorname{sgn}(C^n_\lambda)=(-1)^{R(\lambda)}.
\]

For `n >= max(5,S(lambda))`, normality gives the sharper version of Remark 3:

\[
\langle C^n_\lambda\rangle=
\begin{cases}
S_n,&R(\lambda)\text{ odd},\\
A_n,&R(\lambda)\text{ even}.
\end{cases}
\]

The generating class is inverse-closed, because inversion preserves cycle type, so this is
an undirected Cayley graph.

## 2. Theorem-by-theorem audit

### Theorem 2: correct, but it should be stronger and integral

Transposition length is subadditive:

\[
R(\sigma\tau)\le R(\sigma)+R(\tau).
\]

Every generator has transposition length `R(lambda)`. Consequently

\[
\ell_\lambda(\sigma)\ge
\left\lceil\frac{R(\sigma)}{R(\lambda)}\right\rceil.
\]

Maximizing `R(sigma)` in the generated group gives

\[
D^n_\lambda\ge
\begin{cases}
\left\lceil\dfrac{n-1}{R(\lambda)}\right\rceil,
&\langle C^n_\lambda\rangle=S_n,\\[6pt]
\left\lceil\dfrac{2\lfloor(n-1)/2\rfloor}{R(\lambda)}\right\rceil,
&\langle C^n_\lambda\rangle=A_n.
\end{cases}
\]

The second numerator is `n-1` for odd `n` and `n-2` for even `n`. Thus the paper's
alternating-group bound `(n-2)/R` throws away one unit when `n` is odd.

There is an additional parity refinement in the symmetric case. Here `R(lambda)` is odd,
so a word of length `r` has sign `(-1)^r`. If a target has transposition length `t`, then
`r` must have the same parity as `t`. Define

\[
p_R(t)=\min\{r\ge0:rR\ge t,\ r\equiv t\pmod2\}.
\]

Since every `0 <= t <= n-1` occurs as the transposition length of a permutation,

\[
D^n_\lambda\ge
\max\{p_R(t_{\rm even}),p_R(t_{\rm odd})\},
\]

where `t_even` and `t_odd` are the largest even and odd integers at most `n-1`.
This explains several one-step periodic corrections seen in the exact computations.

### Remark 3: correct in the stated stable range

The subgroup generated by an `S_n`-conjugacy class is normal in `S_n`. For `n >= 5`, a
nontrivial such subgroup is `A_n` or `S_n`; parity decides which. The paper should also
state `n >= S(lambda)`.

### Theorem 4: plausible and repairable, but incomplete as written

The intended “parallel block” construction is good. If types `lambda` and `mu` have
asymptotic capacities `K` and `K'`, put roughly `alpha K` points in one block and
`alpha K'` in another, solve the two block permutations using the same number of factors,
and pair corresponding disjoint factors. Each pair has type `lambda+mu`.

The written proof needs the following repairs.

#### Gap A: block membership/parity

The proof constructs arbitrary block permutations `tau` and `tau'` and invokes the
diameter hypotheses. If `C_lambda` is even, however, it generates only the alternating
group on its block; an odd `tau` is not representable at all.

Repair: when closing the cycle fragment at a block boundary, choose the closing
permutation with the required parity. If its parity is wrong, compose it with a
transposition supported on two points of that block. This changes agreement with the
target on only `O(1)` additional points. Do this independently for both blocks.

#### Gap B: bounded support does not automatically mean bounded word length

The proof says that because `sigma rho^{-1}` moves only `O(1)` points, it needs only
`O(1)` generators. This is true here, but it requires a lemma.

**Stabilization lemma.** Fix a nontrivial type `nu` and an integer `M`. There is a
constant `B(nu,M)` such that, for every sufficiently large `n`, every element of
`<C^n_nu>` with support at most `M` has `C^n_nu`-word length at most `B(nu,M)`.

Proof: conjugate the small support into a fixed set of size `N0 >= max(5,M+S(nu))`.
The corresponding element lies in the appropriate `A_N0` or `S_N0`, whose diameter with
respect to `C^N0_nu` is finite. Embed that fixed word back into `S_n`.

#### Gap C: a dimension typo/ambiguity

When `tau` acts on `floor(alpha K)` points, the applicable hypothesis is the bound for
`D^{floor(alpha K)}_lambda`, not `D^n_lambda`. The intended use is clear but should be
written explicitly.

#### Gap D: small support and fixed points

The reduction “without loss, `n=ell_s`” replaces `n` by the support size of `sigma`.
This is harmless only after splitting off the case of bounded support, which is handled by
the stabilization lemma. If the support tends to infinity, the reduced bound is stronger
than the original one because `support(sigma) <= n`.

#### Simplified repaired proof

Let `a=floor(n/(K+K'))`, `p=floor(aK)`, and `q=floor(aK')`; fewer than three points are
left over. Relabel the cycles of the target consecutively and cut after positions `p` and
`p+q`. Closing the at most two cut cycle fragments gives block permutations that differ
from the target at only `O(1)` points. Correct their parity locally when necessary.

Use the two diameter hypotheses to write the block permutations in at most `a+C` factors.
Pad by inverse pairs. If the two word-length parities differ, multiply the shorter target
by one generator before padding; this changes only a fixed number of points. Now both
words have a common length `L=a+O(1)`. Pair their factors. Because the paired factors have
disjoint supports, every pair has cycle type `lambda+mu`. The resulting product differs
from the target on only `O(1)` points, and the stabilization lemma repairs that residual in
`O(1)` further moves. Therefore

\[
D^n_{\lambda+\mu}\le\frac{n}{K+K'}+O(1).
\]

### Lemma 5: correct

This is the standard exact transposition length result:

\[
\ell_{C_2}(\sigma)=n-c(\sigma),\qquad D^n_{C_2}=n-1.
\]

### Lemma 6: correct, but the missing proof is essential

Herzog and Reid proved the exact 3-cycle length formula. If the nontrivial disjoint cycle
lengths of `sigma in A_n` are `c_1,...,c_r`, then

\[
\ell_{C_3}(\sigma)=\sum_{i=1}^r
\left\lceil\frac{c_i-1}{2}\right\rceil
=\sum_{i=1}^r\left\lfloor\frac{c_i}{2}\right\rfloor.
\]

This immediately gives

\[
D^n_{C_3}=\left\lfloor\frac n2\right\rfloor.
\]

For odd `n`, an `n`-cycle attains the bound. For even `n`, a suitable even permutation
built from even cycles attains it: use all transpositions when `n` is divisible by 4, and
one 4-cycle plus `(n-4)/2` transpositions when `n` is 2 modulo 4.

The exact factor-length result is in [Herzog and Reid, *Representation of permutations as
products of cycles of fixed length* (1976)](https://doi.org/10.1017/S1446788700014774).

### Theorem 7: leading-term equivalence is correct after Theorem 4 is repaired

A generator of type `d lambda` is the product of `d` disjoint generators of type
`lambda`. This gives the useful exact one-sided comparison

\[
D^n_\lambda\le dD^n_{d\lambda}+1.
\]

The `+1` is needed only when `C_lambda` is odd and `d` is even: `C_{d lambda}` generates
`A_n`, so an odd target is first made even using one `lambda`-generator.

The opposite direction is asymptotic and comes from repeated use of Theorem 4. Together
these prove the stated scaling of the leading coefficient. The lower-bound statements
should use `-O(1)`, not `+O(1)`.

### Corollary 8: correct after the preceding repairs

For 2- and 3-cycles, the capacity per component equals its transposition rank:

\[
K_2=1,\qquad K_3=2.
\]

Scaling and adding capacities gives the upper bound with denominator `m_2+2m_3`, while
Theorem 2 gives the matching lower bound. The statement should say “nonnegative integers
not both zero,” not “positive integers ... not both zero.”

## 3. Exact checks on small examples

### Method

The word metric is constant on conjugacy classes. I therefore did not BFS over all `n!`
permutations. Instead, I used integer partitions of `n` as quotient states:

1. choose one representative of each cycle type;
2. multiply it by every generator in `C^n_lambda`;
3. retain only the cycle type of the product;
4. BFS from the identity partition.

This quotient BFS is exact: conjugating both the current permutation and a generator does
not change the set of reachable output cycle types. Generator counts were checked against

\[
|C^n_\lambda|=
\frac{n!}{(n-S(\lambda))!\prod_j j^{m_j}m_j!}.
\]

### Results

| generator type | tested `n` | exact diameters in order |
|---|---:|---|
| `2` | 3-8 | `2,3,4,5,6,7` |
| `3` | 3-10 | `1,2,2,3,3,4,4,5` |
| `2^2` | 4-12 | `1,2,2,3,3,4,4,5,5` |
| `3^2` | 6-14 | `3,2,2,2,3,3,3,3,4` |
| `2*3` | 5-12 | `3,3,3,3,4,4,4,5` |
| `2^2*3` | 7-12 | `2,2,2,2,3,3` |
| `5` | 7-12 | `2,3,3,3,4,4` |
| `7` | 9-12 | `2,2,3,3` |

Additional check: the 5-cycle diameter at `n=16` is `6`, exactly matching the period-12
formula below; the transposition-rank lower bound there is only `4`.

Important conclusions:

- The asymptotic corollary for 2- and 3-cycles is consistent with every exact check.
- Diameters need not be monotone before the stable range: for type `3^2`, the diameter
  falls from `3` at `n=6` to `2` at `n=7`. Extra fixed points can make factorizations
  easier.
- Exact diameter is not a function of `R(lambda)` alone. Both `3^2` and `2^2*3` have
  `R=4`, but at `n=10` their diameters are respectively `3` and `2`.
- Parity matters. For an odd generator type of rank `R`, a target of transposition length
  `t` requires a word length congruent to `t` modulo 2.
- Small-support obstructions matter. If an odd generator is not a transposition, a single
  transposition cannot have length 1 or 2, hence has length at least 3.

For the pure scaled families, the data suggests the following useful conjectures (not
proved here):

Let `T_m(n)` contain all `0 <= t <= n-1` when `m` is odd, and only the even such `t`
when `m` is even. Then

\[
D^n_{2^m}\stackrel{?}=\max_{t\in T_m(n)}
\min\{r:rm\ge t,\ rm\equiv t\pmod2\}
\quad\text{after a type-dependent stable threshold},
\]

with the parity condition relevant when `m` is odd, and

\[
D^n_{3^m}\stackrel{?}=\left\lceil
\frac{\lfloor n/2\rfloor}{m}\right\rceil
\]

after a type-dependent stable threshold. For `m=2`, the latter holds in the exact tests
for `7 <= n <= 14`; `n=6` is exceptional.

## 4. Closely related literature

The paper's subject is usually called the **power/covering number of a conjugacy class**.
The Cayley diameter is the least class power that covers the generated group.

1. [Herzog and Reid (1976)](https://doi.org/10.1017/S1446788700014774) study the minimum
   number of fixed-length cycles representing a permutation and give exact results for
   cycle lengths 2, 3, and 4. This directly supplies the missing Lemma 6.
2. [Bertram and Herzog (2001), *Powers of cycle-classes in symmetric groups*](https://doi.org/10.1006/jcta.2000.3131)
   determine several exact small-power covering thresholds.
3. [Herzog, Kaplan, and Lev (2008)](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jcta.2008.01.010)
   study exactly the diameter/covering problem for `l`-cycle classes in alternating
   groups.
4. [Kishnani, Kundu, and Mishra (2022), part I](https://arxiv.org/abs/2207.03165) and
   [part II](https://arxiv.org/abs/2210.15354) determine the exact covering threshold
   `n(k,l)` for fixed-length cycle classes. Part II also shows that the earlier proposed
   `2kl/3` formula fails by a term linear in `k` when `3` does not divide `l`.
5. [Moran (1976)](https://doi.org/10.1016/0012-365X(76)90110-2) gives necessary and
   sufficient conditions for products of two prescribed involution/reflection classes,
   relevant to the families `2^m`.
6. [Liebeck and Shalev (2001)](https://annals.math.princeton.edu/articles/11021) give
   sharp order-of-magnitude diameter bounds for finite simple groups generated by normal
   subsets. This is broader but less exact than the fixed-cycle-type problem here.

The product of conjugacy-class sums in the center of the symmetric-group algebra gives an
alternative exact computational route via connection coefficients. It is natural for
fixed powers, but standard class-algebra stability does not by itself prove eventual
quasipolynomiality when the required power grows linearly with `n`.

## 5. Exact quasi-linear diameter formulas for single odd cycles

This is the main exact improvement available from the literature.

Let `C_l` be the class of `l`-cycles with `l` odd, and let `D_l(n)` be the diameter of
`Cay(A_n,C_l)`. Let `n(k,l)` be the largest `n` for which every element of `A_n` is a
product of `k` `l`-cycles. For odd `l`, the representable sets are nested as `k` grows, so

\[
D_l(n)=\min\{k:n\le n(k,l)\}.
\]

For `l=3`,

\[
D_3(n)=\left\lfloor\frac n2\right\rfloor.
\]

Now assume `l>3` is odd and put

\[
a=\left\lfloor\frac{2l}{3}\right\rfloor.
\]

For `n >= l+2`, inversion of the exact covering formulas gives:

### `l = 1 (mod 3)`

\[
\boxed{D_l(n)=\left\lceil\frac{n-2}{a}\right\rceil},
\qquad a=\frac{2(l-1)}3.
\]

This is a degree-one quasipolynomial of period dividing `a`.

### `l = 2 (mod 3)`

\[
\boxed{
D_l(n)=
\left\lceil\frac{n-1}{a}\right\rceil
+\mathbf 1_{\,n\equiv a+1\pmod{4a}}
},
\qquad a=\frac{2l-1}{3}.
\]

This has period dividing `4a`.

### `l = 0 (mod 3)`, `l >= 9`

\[
\boxed{
D_l(n)=
\left\lceil\frac{n-1}{a}\right\rceil
+\mathbf 1_{\,n\equiv a+1\pmod{2a}}
},
\qquad a=\frac{2l}{3}.
\]

This has period dividing `2a`. The case `l=3` is exceptional and is given separately
above.

### Concrete examples

\[
D_5(n)=
\left\lceil\frac{n-1}{3}\right\rceil
+\mathbf 1_{\,n\equiv4\pmod{12}}
\quad(n\ge7),
\]

\[
D_7(n)=\left\lceil\frac{n-2}{4}\right\rceil
\quad(n\ge9),
\]

\[
D_9(n)=
\left\lceil\frac{n-1}{6}\right\rceil
+\mathbf 1_{\,n\equiv7\pmod{12}}
\quad(n\ge11).
\]

These formulas show why a generalization based only on transposition rank cannot work.
For a single `l`-cycle, the paper's lower bound predicts capacity `l-1`, whereas the exact
asymptotic capacity for `l>=3` is

\[
\kappa_l=\left\lfloor\frac{2l}{3}\right\rfloor.
\]

For example, 5-cycles have `kappa_5=3`, not `4`.

For even `l`, an `l`-cycle is odd and generates `S_n`. The cited covering formulas with
even `k` cover `A_n`. If `E_l(n)` is the least such even `k`, then

\[
E_l(n)\le D_{S_n,C_l}(n)\le E_l(n)+1,
\]

so the same leading capacity `floor(2l/3)` holds, although an exact all-parity formula
requires a separate analysis of the odd coset.

## 6. A rigorous broader generalization

Theorem 4 and the single-cycle literature combine to give a useful general bracket.
For fixed type `lambda=(2^{m_2}\cdots k^{m_k})`, define

\[
K_0(\lambda)=\sum_{j\ge2}m_j\left\lfloor\frac{2j}{3}\right\rfloor,
\qquad
R(\lambda)=\sum_{j\ge2}(j-1)m_j.
\]

Using the known single-cycle asymptotics, Theorem 7 for multiplicities, and repeated
Theorem 4 for sums gives

\[
\boxed{
\frac{n}{R(\lambda)}-O(1)
\le D^n_\lambda
\le \frac{n}{K_0(\lambda)}+O(1)
}.
\]

For types using only 2- and 3-cycles,

\[
\left\lfloor\frac{2\cdot2}{3}\right\rfloor=1=2-1,
\qquad
\left\lfloor\frac{2\cdot3}{3}\right\rfloor=2=3-1,
\]

so `K_0(lambda)=R(lambda)` and the two bounds coincide. This explains structurally why
Corollary 8 works and why the same argument stops being exact at cycle length 4.

It is tempting to conjecture that `D^n_lambda` is eventually quasi-linear for every fixed
cycle type. The computations and the single-cycle theorems support that conjecture, but
the current PDF does not prove it. Nor does Theorem 4 preserve enough residue information
to imply it. A serious route would be:

1. identify a finite list of extremal target cycle patterns;
2. characterize membership in `(C^n_lambda)^r` by linear inequalities and congruences in
   their cycle counts;
3. invert those Presburger-type conditions in `r` and `n`.

That route would naturally produce eventual quasipolynomials. The recent cycle-class
papers succeed precisely by finding such extremal patterns and congruence obstructions.

## 7. Recommended revised theorem package

For a short, clean paper, I would reorganize the argument as follows.

1. Define support `S(lambda)`, index `R(lambda)`, word length, and stable range.
2. State the parity classification of the generated group.
3. Prove the exact index lower bound with ceilings and the word-parity refinement.
4. Prove the stabilization lemma for bounded-support residuals.
5. State and prove the repaired capacity-addition theorem using the two-block construction.
6. Quote or prove the exact transposition and 3-cycle length formulas.
7. Derive the 2/3-cycle corollary in three lines.
8. Add a literature section identifying the result as a conjugacy-class covering problem.
9. Add the exact single-odd-cycle quasipolynomials above as a comparison showing both the
   strength and the limit of the method.

The best concise interpretation is: **the paper proves an addition law for asymptotic
covering capacities; transpositions and 3-cycles are special because their capacity equals
their transposition rank.**
